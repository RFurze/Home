profile

Research profile

Computational engineer and physicist with a decade of professional experience spanning scientific research, engineering and accountancy. I hold an integrated Master’s degree in Theoretical Physics and returned to academia after ten years as a Chartered Accountant to complete an MSc in Medical Engineering. I am now finishing a PhD in the EPSRC Centre for Doctoral Training in Fluid Dynamics at the University of Leeds.

My PhD involves developing a novel multiscale model to predict friction and lubrication in total hip replacement bearings, coupling microscale surface physics to macroscale elastohydrodynamic simulations via the Heterogeneous Multiscale Method. The framework is implemented using the finite element method in FEniCS and NGSolve and evaluated through HPC parameter studies. The project is supervised across computer science, mechanical engineering and industry. Expected completion March 2027.

The intervening decade was spent diagnosing and fixing complex technical and operational problems in large organisations: identifying the underlying problem, developing and implementing solutions, delivering to deadlines, and keeping technical and non-technical stakeholders engaged throughout the process and its outcomes.

publications

Publications

R. Furze, P. K. Jimack, M. Bryant, M. Al-Hajjar, L. Wang and G. de Boer. A transient multiscale framework for elastohydrodynamic lubrication of rough ball-in-socket bearings. Accepted for publication in Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering.

A fully transient Heterogeneous Multiscale Method for elastohydrodynamic lubrication in conformal bearings. Microscale simulations of surface roughness provide corrections to the macroscale flow and load-bearing response, enabling friction and lubrication to be predicted without resolving the surface geometry throughout the full bearing simulation. The multiscale model matches a high-resolution deterministic solution to within 3% in minimum film thickness and 3% in pressure at a thirteenfold reduction in computational cost, demonstrated under ASTM F3143 reciprocating hip loading and velocity profiles.
